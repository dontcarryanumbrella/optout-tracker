        01 · Browsers and cookies

        Use a browser that blocks tracking

        Tracking cookies and scripts follow you from site to site and feed the same profiles that data brokers sell. A browser that blocks them by default does most of the work for you.

        Firefox

        Blocks known trackers and third-party cookies by default, and still runs the full uBlock Origin ad blocker.

        Get Firefox ↗

        Brave

        Blocks ads and trackers without any setup, and sends the Global Privacy Control signal by default.

        Get Brave ↗

        DuckDuckGo browser

        Blocks trackers, sends Global Privacy Control by default, and clears your tabs and data with one tap of its Fire button.

        Get DuckDuckGo ↗

        Tor Browser

        The strongest protection: it hides your location and makes you look like every other Tor user. Pages load more slowly, and some sites block it.

        Get Tor Browser ↗

        02 · Global Privacy Control

        Tell every site not to sell your data

        Global Privacy Control (GPC) is a browser setting that sends a do-not-sell-or-share signal to every site you visit. In about a dozen states, including California, Colorado, Connecticut, New Jersey, Oregon and Texas, companies covered by the state’s privacy law must treat it as an opt-out. Elsewhere, some sites honor it anyway.

        Checking whether your browser sends it…

        Already on

        Brave and the DuckDuckGo browser send GPC by default. Firefox sends it in private windows.

        Firefox

        Turn it on for every window:

        Settings › Privacy & Security › Website Privacy Preferences › Tell websites not to sell or share my data

        Chrome, Edge and Safari

        None of them has a built-in setting yet, so add an extension that sends it, like Privacy Badger. A California law requires browsers to offer a built-in opt-out signal starting January 1, 2027.

        Privacy Badger ↗

        Check it works

        The GPC project’s site tells you whether it detects the signal from your browser. So does the line above.

        globalprivacycontrol.org ↗

        07 · Calls, mail and credit

        Cut down junk calls, mail and credit offers

        Telemarketers, mailing lists and credit offers draw on the same data. These official services shut off a lot of it.

        Do Not Call Registry

        Stops most telemarketing sales calls. It doesn’t stop political calls, charities, debt collectors or scammers.

        donotcall.gov ↗

        Prescreened offers

        The credit bureaus’ official site stops “you’re pre-approved” credit card and insurance offers, for five years online or for good by mail.

        OptOutPrescreen ↗

        Marketing mail

        DMAchoice cuts catalogs and marketing mail from companies that use its list, and can stop mail addressed to someone who has died.

        DMAchoice ↗

        Freeze your credit

        A free freeze at all three bureaus stops anyone from opening new credit in your name. You can lift it whenever you apply for something.

        Equifax ↗Experian ↗TransUnion ↗

        If your identity is stolen

        The FTC’s IdentityTheft.gov builds a step-by-step recovery plan and the official report you’ll need.

        IdentityTheft.gov ↗
        08 · Accounts and breaches

        Give out less in the first place

        Every sign-up form is a new copy of your details. Share less of them, and find out when they leak.

        Check for breaches

        Have I Been Pwned shows which data breaches included your email or phone number, and can alert you to new ones.

        Have I Been Pwned ↗

        Email aliases

        Give each site its own forwarding address. If one starts getting spam or turns up in a breach, turn it off without changing your real email.

        Firefox Relay ↗DuckDuckGo Email ↗SimpleLogin ↗

        iPhone users with iCloud+ have Hide My Email built in.

        A password manager

        Use a unique password for every account, so one breach doesn’t unlock the rest. Bitwarden is free and open source. Turn on passkeys or two-step sign-in where you can.

        Bitwarden ↗

        App permissions

        Apps that know your precise location can sell where you live and work. Set location to “While using” or off, and review which apps have contacts and photos.

        09 · If your safety is at risk

        If someone is trying to find you

        Opting out helps, but brokers can relist you from public records. If you’re escaping abuse or being stalked, these programs protect your address at the source.

        Address confidentiality programs

        Most states give survivors of domestic violence, stalking and sexual assault a substitute address to use on public records, mail and voter rolls, so your home address never reaches them. A few states have no program.

        Programs by state ↗

        National Domestic Violence Hotline

        Free, confidential help around the clock: call 800-799-7233 or text START to 88788.

        thehotline.org ↗

        Technology safety

        If someone may be monitoring your phone or computer, use a device they can’t reach. The Safety Net project explains how to check and what to do.

        techsafety.org ↗

        Who operates the tracker

        I’m Tom Sun, the creator and operator of the Opt-Out Tracker, a free, noncommercial project developed independently as part of my interests in public policy and computer science. You can contact me at tom.sun@duke.edu.

        This policy covers the entire Opt-Out Tracker website, including the checklist, Resources page, interactive features and downloads. The tracker runs in your browser without an account. It does not send the personal details, notes or progress you enter to me. Information you choose to email me, requests handled by the website’s host, and information you send to other services are separate and are explained below.

        Information stored in your browser

        The tracker uses your browser’s local storage to remember information on the device and browser you are using.

        Personal details and drafts. These include your name, other names, email address, phone number, street address, city and ZIP code, along with listing links, notes and changes you make to request emails. The tracker uses this information to help you prepare requests and organize your records. Every field is optional. Each request draft lists the details that broker’s opt-out asks for, using what you’ve saved and leaving a bracketed placeholder for anything you haven’t, so you can fill it in before sending. Please don’t save your date of birth or government ID numbers in the tracker; if a broker asks for one, type it into that email yourself. If you add a phone number, it appears in the signature of each draft. New personal details are saved to local storage only after you set a passphrase and only in encrypted form.

        Progress and settings. These include each site’s status and dates, your selected state, timing and reminder preferences, and your preferred mail service. The tracker stores these without encryption so it can display your checklist while your personal details are locked. These records do not include the name or contact details you enter in the personal-details fields, but they can reveal which sites you have checked and the progress of your requests.

        Display preferences. The tracker also stores your theme and sound preferences without encryption.

        The tracker does not automatically synchronize these records between devices or send them to a database I operate. Local storage is used when you use the corresponding features or save preferences.

        Encryption and its limits

        Saved personal details are encrypted using AES-256-GCM. The encryption key is derived from your passphrase using PBKDF2-SHA-256 with 600,000 iterations. Your passphrase is not saved by the tracker or sent to me.

        While your details are unlocked, the tracker keeps the key in memory so it can display and use those details. It releases its reference to the key when you press Lock, reload the page or reach your chosen idle-lock time. This does not erase copies you have already placed in your clipboard, an email draft or another application.

        If your browser cannot perform the required encryption, the tracker keeps newly entered personal details in memory for the current page session instead of saving them to local storage. Closing or reloading the page discards those unsaved details.

        An earlier version may have saved personal details without encryption. Those existing records remain unencrypted until you choose to encrypt or delete them. The tracker prompts you to make that choice.

        Encryption helps protect stored information, but no website or device can guarantee complete security. It does not protect information displayed while the tracker is unlocked from someone who can access your device, or from malicious software with access to your browser. If you forget your passphrase, I cannot recover it or decrypt your saved details.

        Features that run on your device

        The Resources page includes links and information about browsers, cookies, Global Privacy Control, data-broker registries, BADBOOL, search-result removal and AI services’ privacy controls. Viewing these resources does not connect you to the listed providers. Following a link opens the provider’s website.

        The optional music is generated by code in your browser after you choose to play it. It is not streamed from an external music service. The animations and their supporting code also run locally.

        CSV downloads and calendar files are generated in your browser. Creating them does not upload their contents to me or to an external service. If you later share, import or upload a file, the recipient or service may receive its contents. The broker-list CSV is separate from a backup of your tracker records.

        The Global Privacy Control (GPC) check reads the setting your browser makes available to this page. The check happens locally and does not send its result to me or an external testing service. It does not enable GPC, change your browser settings or verify whether another website honors the signal.

        When information leaves the tracker

        Email drafts. Selecting “Open in Gmail” or “Open in Outlook” opens a link containing the draft and any personal details included in it. Google or Microsoft receives that information when the link opens, even if you never send the email. “Open in Gmail” also includes the email address you entered, so Gmail can open the matching account. The link may also remain in browser history. Selecting “My mail app” passes the draft to your chosen email application, which may store or synchronize it according to its own settings. “Copy email” places the draft on your clipboard instead.

        The tracker does not send the email to the broker. You decide whether to press Send in your email service; when you do, the recipient receives the message and the sender information supplied by your email service.

        Calendar reminders. A Google Calendar link passes the reminder’s title, date and the tracker’s website address to Google. Calendar files for Apple Calendar or Outlook are generated on your device. Importing or synchronizing those files may share their contents with your calendar provider.

        External websites. Links to broker search pages, opt-out forms, California’s DROP and other resources open independently operated services. Information you enter on those services goes to their operators. The tracker instructs your browser not to send its page address as a referrer when following those links; the destination can still receive your IP address and other information associated with the visit.

Encrypted details remain encrypted in that backup, while progress remains readable. You control where you paste or store the backup. Clipboard history, synchronization, email and cloud-storage services may retain copies according to their settings. Other copying features, such as “Copy email,” may contain readable personal information.
